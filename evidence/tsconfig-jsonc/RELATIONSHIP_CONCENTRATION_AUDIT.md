# Recovered-edge concentration audit

## Question

The main Lexical result reports **4,256 verified import instances** lost
as-is and restored by the strict-JSON control. That number should not be read
as 4,256 independent failures, questions, or user impacts. This audit asks two
narrow accounting questions:

1. How many of those instances are merely repeated source→target pairs?
2. How concentrated are the recovered edges in a few hub/barrel targets?

Machine-readable output:
`relationship_concentration_audit.json`, generated by
`reproduction/relationship_concentration_audit.py` from the committed
verified-relationship and direct-selection-omission JSONL files.

## Result

| Dataset | Import instances | Distinct source→target pairs | Duplicate pair instances beyond the first | Distinct sources | Distinct direct targets |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Lexical | 4,256 | 4,218 | 38 (99.1071% pair retention) | 1,290 | 101 |
| ng-matero | 111 | 111 | 0 | 92 | 6 |

So duplicate source→target pairs explain almost none of the instance count.
That is **not** the same as independence: the direct targets are strongly
hub-concentrated.

For Lexical, on a distinct-pair basis:

- the top direct target, `packages/lexical/src/index.ts`, receives 1,195
  distinct source files (28.331% of recovered pairs);
- the top 5 targets receive 58.606%, and the top 10 receive 73.779%;
- the sum of squared target shares is 0.116124, reciprocal **8.611455
  effective targets**—a concentration summary, not a claim that only nine
  files matter;
- source out-degree is comparatively diffuse: median 3, p90 6, maximum 17
  distinct recovered targets per source;
- target in-degree is skewed: median 6, p90 93, p95 146, maximum 1,195
  distinct recovered sources per target.

For ng-matero, the top target (`src/app/shared/index.ts`) receives 67 of 111
distinct pairs (60.3604%); the six targets have an effective target count of
2.371704 under the same reciprocal-squared-shares summary.

## Spread is nevertheless repository-wide

Lexical's recovered pairs span:

- 41 source areas and 35 target areas under the audit's
  `packages/<package>` accounting (with non-package trees kept at their first
  path component);
- 401 distinct source-area→target-area combinations;
- 3,620 cross-area pairs, 85.8227% of distinct recovered pairs.

ng-matero's recovered pairs span 8 source areas, 4 target areas, and 18
area pairs; 101/111 (90.991%) cross the audit's `src/app/<area>` boundary.
The largest ng-matero flow is `src/app/routes → src/app/shared` (54 distinct
pairs).

These are descriptive repository-accounting facts. They show that the defect
is not confined to one directory, but they do not convert area counts into a
prevalence estimate or independent user impact.

## Limits

- This is **not** a whole-repository graph reconstruction. Edges that already
  resolved as-is are not inputs, so no claim is made about whole-graph
  components, shortest paths, or global reachability.
- Distinct pairs remove one specific inflation mechanism. They do not make
  hub/barrel evidence independent; the exhaustive symbol-evidence audit has
  the same caveat and reports its defining-file concentration separately.
- Effective target count is only the reciprocal of summed squared target
  shares. It is useful for comparing concentration, not a substitute for the
  full target distribution retained in JSON.
- The audit is deterministic over committed evidence and requires neither a
  fresh UnvibeCode run nor network access.
